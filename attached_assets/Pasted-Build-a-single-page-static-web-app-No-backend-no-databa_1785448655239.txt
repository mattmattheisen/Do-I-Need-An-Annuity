PURPOSE
A retirement income decision tool for clients of Gambit Capital Management, LLC.
It helps someone decide whether an immediate income annuity fits their situation,
and if so, how much of their net worth should reasonably go into one.

DESIGN
- Aesthetic: clean institutional financial application (think Fidelity or Vanguard).
- Single accent color: emerald green. Backgrounds white and light gray only.
  No secondary accent colors.
- No emojis. No decorative icons. Text and clean rules only.
- Generous whitespace, clearly labeled fields, large readable type.
- Logo (I'll upload the image) in the top-left header.
- Mobile responsive.

FLOW
A step-by-step wizard. One screen at a time, a single "Next" button per screen,
a "Back" link. Progress indicator showing step X of 3. No screen should ask for
more than 4 inputs. The user should never have to do math.

Screen 1 — "Your situation"
- Current age (number)
- Age you expect to live to (number, default 90, with helper text: "Most people
  underestimate this. Average 65-year-old lives to about 85; half live longer.")
- Guaranteed annual income you already have from Social Security and any pension
  (dollar input)
- Two yes/no toggles:
  - "Is leaving money to heirs important to you?"
  - "Are you concerned about future healthcare or long-term care costs?"

Screen 2 — "Your finances"
- Total investable assets (dollar input)
- Annual spending you want to support in retirement (dollar input)
- One slider: "How comfortable are you with market ups and downs?"
  (5 positions, labeled Very uncomfortable → Very comfortable)

Screen 3 — "Your results"
See RESULTS below.

SUITABILITY SCORE
Compute a 0–100 score from four equally weighted 0–25 components. Show the total
prominently, then a short breakdown of each component with a one-line plain-English
explanation of why it scored the way it did.

1. Longevity (0-25): longer expected lifespan = higher score.
   Scale from expected-age 80 (0 pts) to 100 (25 pts), linear.

2. Income gap (0-25): compute gap = spending goal − existing guaranteed income.
   Express as a percentage of the spending goal. 0% gap = 0 pts. 60%+ gap = 25 pts,
   linear in between.

3. Flexibility need (0-25): start at 25 and subtract.
   −10 if leaving money to heirs is important.
   −10 if concerned about healthcare/long-term care costs.
   −5 if the annuity purchase would exceed 35% of investable assets.

4. Behavioral fit (0-25): from the market-comfort slider, inverted —
   "very uncomfortable" = 25 pts, "very comfortable" = 0 pts.

Interpretation bands, displayed with the score:
0–25   "An annuity is unlikely to be the right tool for your situation."
26–50  "An annuity may make sense for a small portion of your assets."
51–75  "An annuity is worth serious consideration as part of your income plan."
76–100 "An annuity aligns well with your priorities and goals."

RIGHT-SIZED RECOMMENDATION
Advisors are generally limited to recommending no more than 50% of a client's
investable assets into an annuity. Many push clients toward that ceiling. This
tool recommends what actually fits.

Recommended allocation percentage = (suitability score / 100) × 50%, then
capped so the resulting annuity income does not exceed the income gap.
Display:
- Recommended dollar amount into an annuity
- That amount as a percentage of investable assets
- Estimated annual income it would produce (use these approximate immediate
  annuity payout rates by purchase age, single life: 60→5.8%, 65→6.4%,
  70→7.3%, 75→8.5%, 80→10.2%; interpolate between)
- The 50% industry ceiling shown alongside, with a plain-English line noting
  that the ceiling is a regulatory maximum, not a target.

Also show, in a simple two-column comparison:
- What the recommended annuity gives up: liquidity, market participation on that
  portion, and anything left to heirs on that portion.
- What stays flexible: the remaining assets, still invested and accessible.
