Good fixes. Four things before you call this done.

The new penalty may now fire for nearly everyone. Run the threshold backward: the test is gap ÷ payoutRate > 0.35 × assets. At a ~6.5% payout that reduces to gap > 0.023 × assets. For a client with $600k, any income gap above roughly $14,000 trips it. Almost every client who has a gap worth analyzing will have one larger than that — which means flexibility effectively tops out at 20 instead of 25, and the component stops discriminating between clients. Case C dropping to 52 is the first sighting of this.

That's the tradeoff for removing the circularity: the old test was ill-defined, the new one is well-defined but close to constant. Two ways out. Raise the threshold so it identifies genuine concentration rather than ordinary gaps — testing against 60% of assets would mean it fires only when closing the gap would consume most of the portfolio, which is the situation you actually meant to flag. Or take concentration out of the score entirely and handle it where it belongs: the recommendation is already capped at 50%, and you already have a consideration block that warns on concentration. Scoring it a third time is redundant. I'd lean toward the second, but the first is a one-line change.

Either way, sample it before deciding — run twenty plausible client profiles and count how often the penalty fires. If it's eighteen, it isn't measuring anything.
